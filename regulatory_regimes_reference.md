# Regulatory Regimes Reference for Delivery Workflows

## Purpose
This reference summarises how common regulatory regimes translate into workflow controls. It is guidance for workflow design, not legal advice; the compliance function confirms obligations for each project.

## UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018
Processing personal data requires a lawful basis, data minimisation, storage limitation and appropriate security. High risk processing requires a data protection impact assessment before it starts. Workflow controls: a privacy impact assessment gate before personal data processing changes go live, the data protection officer as approver, and periodic retention reviews.

## HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules
United States health data protections covering protected health information. Workflow controls: privacy and security review gates, access reviews of who can view protected health information, and audit logging.

## Sarbanes Oxley Section 404
Management must maintain and assess internal controls over financial reporting. Workflow controls: segregation of duties on changes to financially significant systems, approval by a control owner, and an audit trail of changes retained for several years.

## PCI DSS v4.0
Requirements for entities that store, process or transmit cardholder data. Workflow controls: security review of changes in the cardholder data environment, secure development practices and a periodic review of the scope boundary.

## ISO/IEC 27001
An information security management system standard. Workflow controls: security review of significant changes and evidence of control operation.

## FDA 21 CFR Part 11
Requirements for electronic records and electronic signatures in FDA regulated activities. Workflow controls: validated systems, secure audit trails that record changes without obscuring previous values, and electronic signatures that record the signer, date, time and meaning of the signature.

## IEC 62304 and ISO 13485
IEC 62304 defines lifecycle processes for medical device software scaled by safety classification; ISO 13485 defines quality management system requirements for medical devices. Workflow controls: requirement to risk control traceability, verification and validation records, design history file evidence and controlled release.

## EU Digital Operational Resilience Act
Applies to financial entities in the European Union and requires ICT risk management, classification and reporting of major ICT related incidents, resilience testing and management of third party ICT risk. Workflow controls: incident classification automation, tested rollback plans and resilience testing evidence.

## NIS2 Directive
Applies to essential and important entities across sectors such as energy, transport, health and digital infrastructure. Significant incidents require an early warning to the competent authority or CSIRT within 24 hours of becoming aware, followed by an incident notification within 72 hours. Workflow controls: an incident early warning automation and security review of significant changes.

## FCA Consumer Duty
Requires UK firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers across products and services, price and value, consumer understanding and consumer support. Workflow controls: a customer outcome assessment linked to the release of customer facing changes.

## WCAG 2.2 AA and the Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations
UK public sector websites and mobile applications must meet accessibility requirements and publish an accessibility statement. Workflow controls: an accessibility review gate before public release and assistive technology testing in the definition of done.

## MiFID II record keeping
Investment firms must keep records of services, activities and transactions, generally for five years. Workflow controls: record retention automation on decisions and communications.
