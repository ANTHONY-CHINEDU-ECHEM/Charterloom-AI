# Project Charter: Kestrel Signal

Domain: Insurance. Business unit: Claims.

## Purpose
The purpose of Kestrel Signal is to establish whether machine learning can improve fraud referral decisions in the motor claims triage service, where fewer than one in five referrals currently proves fraudulent.

## Background
Feasibility is unproven; the first weeks will test several competing hypotheses about which claim, policy and network features carry signal. It is not yet clear that the available data supports a reliable solution, because historical fraud labels are sparse and inconsistently recorded. We must establish quickly whether the approach works before committing to a full build.

## Objectives
* Determine within twelve weeks whether a model can lift referral precision by at least 30 percent at the current referral volume.
* If successful, deploy the model to score live claims and route them to the right queue.
* Document fairness and explainability evidence acceptable to the model risk committee.

## Scope
In scope: data exploration, feature engineering, model prototyping, offline evaluation and a shadow deployment.
Out of scope: Changes to the claims handling platform beyond a scoring API. Automated claim decline.

## Team and Stakeholders
The delivery team comprises 5 people organised into 1 team: two data scientists, a machine learning engineer, a claims analyst and a product manager. The planned duration is 20 weeks from mobilisation.

## Constraints and Assumptions
Predictions will be generated by a trained model that must be monitored for drift. Sensitive personal information will be processed and retained under our data policy.

## Regulatory and Compliance Context
The solution falls within scope of UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. The use of automated decision support must also respect the FCA Consumer Duty.
